# Leht — name and identity

## The name

**Leht** — Estonian for *sheet of paper*, *page*, and *leaf*. One short word that means all
three, which is as close to "a PDF" as a natural word gets. The product is Estonian-built,
and the name says so without needing explanation.

- **Pronunciation:** "leht" — like English *let* with a breath after the vowel. Estonians,
  Germans and Americans all land in the same place. There are no diacritics to lose.
- **Written:** always *Leht* in prose, capital L. Never LEHT, never Lehtu, never "Leht PDF"
  as the product name — the format is not part of the name.
- **The binary and the namespace are lowercase `leht`.** The brand is *Leht*.
- **Plural / possessive:** avoid. Say "Leht opens…", not "Leht's". English possessives on a
  foreign word read badly.

## What it is not

- Not an acronym. Don't backronym it.
- Not related to LEHT, the abbreviation for Little Egg Harbor Township, New Jersey, which
  holds `leht.com`. Harmless — a municipality, not a software brand, different trademark
  class.
- Not to be confused with *leht* the Estonian word for newspaper, though the overlap is
  friendly: it makes the name feel like printed matter to Estonian speakers.

## Taglines

- English: **Every PDF, one page at a time.**
- Short form: **Leht — the PDF toolkit.**
- Estonian: **Leht. Kõik su PDF-id.**

Keep the tagline doing the format's work so the name doesn't have to.

## Logo direction

The leaf and the sheet of paper share a silhouette: a rounded rectangle with one corner
turned. A page with a folded corner *is* a leaf shape. That's the mark — one form that
reads as both, no literal tree, no literal Acrobat-style document icon.

Avoid: red (Adobe's colour), gradient-heavy app icons, and anything that looks like a
file-format badge.

## Trademark

Verify before filing — the registries were not reachable when this was written:

- Search **LEHT** in classes **9** (software) and **42** (SaaS) at
  [EUIPO](https://www.euipo.europa.eu/en/search-ip) and [TMview](https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/).
- **File a figurative mark, not a word mark.** EUIPO refuses marks that are descriptive in
  *any* official EU language, and Estonian is one of them — a bare "LEHT" for page-handling
  software risks an Art. 7(1)(c) EUTMR objection. The wordmark in its specific typography,
  with the leaf/page symbol, clears distinctiveness and is still enforceable.
- Rough costs: EUIPO ~€850 for one class; Estonian Patendiamet ~€145 for Estonia only.
